Home · Useful · Funding Gap Calculator

Admitted is one thing.
Funded is another.

Before a US university can issue the I-20 you need for an F-1 visa, it has to see documents proving you can cover a full first year. This works out, school by school, what that year would really cost you — and whether what you can document already covers it.

What the I-20 step actually is. Admission and funding are checked separately. Once a university admits you, it will ask for documentation — personal or sponsor bank statements, a scholarship or grant letter, or an approved education loan — showing funds for its full published first-year cost. Only then does it issue the I-20 that lets you book a visa interview. It is a yes-or-no checkpoint: the paperwork either reaches the number or it doesn't. Knowing that number early is the whole point of this page.

These are estimates, and one office has the final word. Every cost figure here comes from what a university published, carries its source and date, and can change between admissions cycles. Every funding figure comes from what you typed. Nothing on this page is a decision, an approval, or a promise. The exact amount a school will require for your I-20, and exactly which documents it will accept, come only from that university's international student office — ask them directly, early, and in writing.
