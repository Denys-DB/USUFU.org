Deadlines cluster in the autumn and winter of your final school year. You almost always need an F-1 student visa (a J-1 applies to some exchange programs). In order: the university issues your I-20 → you pay the I-901 SEVIS fee (about $350) → complete the DS-160 and pay the visa (MRV) fee (about $185) → book and attend your visa interview → if approved, you may enter the U.S. no earlier than 30 days before your program start date. In everyday American English the two words are used almost interchangeably — students say they're "going to college" even when they attend a university. The technical difference: a university is usually larger, offers both undergraduate and graduate (master's, doctoral) degrees, and does research; a college is often smaller and focused on undergraduate teaching (a "liberal arts college" is a well-regarded example). Confusingly, "college" is also the name for a division inside a university (the "College of Engineering"). For your purposes, both a college and a university grant the four-year bachelor's degree, and one is not automatically better than the other. The genuinely different thing is a community college — a two-year school that grants associate degrees and is often used as a more affordable stepping-stone before transferring to a four-year school (see Guide 02). So don't let the words confuse you: focus on the specific school, not its label.У повсякденній американській англійській ці два слова вживають майже як синоніми — студенти кажуть, що «йдуть у college», навіть коли навчаються в університеті. Технічна різниця така: university зазвичай більший, пропонує і бакалаврські, і магістерські/докторські ступені й веде дослідження; college часто менший і зосереджений на навчанні бакалаврів («liberal arts college» — шанований приклад). До того ж «college» — це ще й назва підрозділу всередині університету («College of Engineering»). Для вас важливо, що і college, і university присуджують чотирирічний ступінь бакалавра, і одне не є автоматично кращим за інше. По-справжньому інше — це community college: дворічний заклад, що присуджує associate degree й часто використовується як доступніша сходинка перед переведенням до чотирирічного закладу (див. Гайд 02). Тож не дайте словам вас заплутати: дивіться на конкретний заклад, а не на його назву.

A U.S. bachelor's degree normally takes four years of full-time study — some students finish in three, others take five, and that's an estimate, not a fixed rule. You'll usually take about four or five courses each semester, with two main semesters a year. A typical week is a mix of lectures, smaller discussion sections or seminars, and labs, plus a large amount of independent work — reading, problem sets, essays and projects — because your grade (your GPA) is built continuously from work across the whole term, not from one final exam. Keeping a full course load matters beyond academics: F-1 status requires it, so never drop below full-time without first talking to your DSO (see Guide 04). Yes, within limits — but this is immigration territory that changes and depends on your field and situation, so treat the following as an overview, not advice, and confirm with your DSO or an immigration attorney. As an F-1 graduate you can apply for OPT (Optional Practical Training): about 12 months of work in your field of study. If your degree is in a designated STEM field, you may apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension — up to roughly 36 months of work authorisation in total. Beyond that, staying longer usually means an employer sponsoring an H-1B work visa, which is capped and awarded by lottery, so it is not guaranteed (a "cap-gap" rule can bridge your status if a petition is filed in time). There are strict limits on unemployment during these periods (currently 90 days on OPT, 150 cumulative on STEM OPT).
